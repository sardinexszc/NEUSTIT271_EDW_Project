## Method

- Every query in [`sample-queries.sql`](sample-queries.sql) runs once to warm the cache, then 5 times under `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)`. Times are server-side planning + execution, in milliseconds.
- Acceptance criterion (NFR2, [business case](business-case.md)): every analytical query completes in under 1000 ms.
- Environment: PostgreSQL 17.11 with default settings on Windows 11, local laptop (12th Gen Intel(R) Core(TM) i5-12500H, 16 logical cores, 16 GB RAM); database and client on the same machine, no other load.
- Data volume (seed 271): fact_damage_assessment 8,806 rows, fact_evacuation_snapshot 31,110 rows, fact_resource_distribution 14,927 rows, fact_response_action 32,347 rows, fact_displacement_outside 21,896 rows, fact_lifeline_disruption 2,677 rows.
- Mart baseline: Q13 reads the event headline figures from `mart.mart_event_summary`; Q01 computes the same figures (ranking events by damage with their human toll) from `dw.fact_damage_assessment`. The ratio of their median times is the speed-up quoted below.
- Caveats: times are from one machine and include no network transfer; a warm cache favours repeated dashboard queries. A mart read takes well under 0.1 ms, so its time, and with it the speed-up, varies between runs (between about 60× and 80× in repeated runs); this report shows the latest run.

## Findings

- **14 of 14 queries** meet the 1000 ms budget on every run; the slowest single run was Q09 at 33.2 ms.
- **Marts pay off:** the event headline read from `mart.mart_event_summary` (Q13, 0.05 ms) is about 78× faster than aggregating the same figures from the fact table (Q01, 3.60 ms). This is why the dashboards read marts, not facts.
- **Sequential scans are the right plan at this size.** With tens of thousands of rows per fact, PostgreSQL reads each table in a few milliseconds and chooses sequential scans over indexes. At national scale (millions of rows) the next step is B-tree indexes on the fact foreign keys most used in filters (`event_key`, `location_key`, date keys) and partitioning facts by event year; this benchmark is the baseline to measure that against.
